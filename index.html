    How assembly works

    The blueprint board by the entrance shows all four steps and fills in as you build, so you can always see what is still open.

    Parts sit on numbered stations: Step 1 first stages and Step 2 upper stages down the left wall, Step 3 payloads across the back, Step 4 fairing on the right. Walk into a bay and a panel lists every option with its specifications. Tab compares them, E collects the selected one.

    The first stages are too heavy to lift, so the overhead crane flies those to the stand while you watch. Everything else you carry.

    The payload and fairing go on top of the stack, so those are fitted from the work platform. Step into the service elevator on the right and press E to ride up; if the car is at the other end, pressing E at the landing calls it to you first.

    Do not step off the car while it is raised. A fall from the platform ends the mission.

    A translucent preview shows exactly where the part will attach. Press E to confirm the placement.

    Your payload choice is simply which bench you walk to. Look directly at any fitted part to read what it is and why it is built that way.

    The Vehicle Analysis board is live. Watch the Δv figure as you add parts: two stages alone give about 13 900 m/s, and the eight-tonne telescope takes roughly 3 000 m/s of that away. Reaching orbit costs about 9 400 m/s, so the margin bar is what tells you whether this vehicle can fly the mission.
